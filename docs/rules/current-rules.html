Document Scope

This document records the current game design, including unresolved and candidate rules. The shared feature manifest and the in-app rulebook are canonical for what the digital game currently ships as playable. Changes that overlap game design and shipped behavior must update both this document and the feature manifest in the same change.

Pitch

Players build and exploit a stargate network to connect distant groups, move influence, and create value from routes that other players may also need.

Players

Player count: 3-8.

Target play time: unresolved.

Objective

Unresolved. Candidate objective: score the most points through profitable connections, fulfilled contracts, and durable network position.

Components

  • Middle exoplanets: 2-4.
  • Selection cards: each player has one card for each player, including themself; one card for each exoplanet in the middle; and one pause card.
  • Board or map: unresolved.
  • Stargate pieces: unresolved.
  • Dollars and resources are public balances in the online game; their physical components are unresolved.
  • Module cards: each exoplanet has an empty deck and discard pile in the current game.
  • Factory slots: each exoplanet begins with three; home planets have no slot limit.
  • Player aids: unresolved.

Setup

  1. Place the board or map.
  2. Place 2-4 exoplanets in the middle.
  3. Give each player their selection cards.
  4. Give each player one held copy of each starting module: Solar Farm, Farm, Mine, Smelter, MRE Factory, and Training Center.
  5. Give each player $20, 6 Energy, 1 Food, 8 Metal, and 2 MRE. Players begin with no Ore or Teams.
  6. Give each exoplanet three empty factory slots.
  7. Leave each exoplanet's module deck and discard pile empty.
  8. Give each player their other unresolved starting pieces.
  9. Prepare other card decks, markets, or public objectives.
  10. Choose a first player.

The wider board, starting pieces, markets, objectives, and first-player procedure remain unresolved.

Modules and Factories

Modules

Every module has a name, owner, type, construction cost, running cost, optional inputs, and outputs. A module gains its owner when it is installed in a factory at a home planet or exoplanet.

Factory Slots and Factories

  • A home planet has any number of factory slots.
  • Each exoplanet begins with three empty factory slots.
  • Installing a module into an empty slot forms a factory.
  • A factory contains one or more modules, and all modules in it must have the same type.
  • Any number of modules of that type may stack in the factory.
  • Modules retain their installation order from oldest to newest.
  • Factories remain in placement order and cannot be moved after construction.

An empty slot is not a factory. It becomes a factory when its first module is installed; empty slots never affect production multipliers.

Running a Factory

  • A player may run factories when they connect to themself or to an exoplanet.
  • To run a factory, begin with its oldest installed module.
  • After running that module, the player chooses either to stop that factory or run the next-oldest module.
  • Repeat until the player stops or every installed module has run.
  • A player cannot skip an older module to run a newer module in the same factory.
  • There is no stated limit on how many installed modules in one factory may run.

The player chooses which unrun factory receives each multiplier. The first chosen factory uses 1× dollar running costs, the second uses 2×, the third uses 3×, and so on. Resource inputs are never multiplied, and earlier factories keep their assigned multipliers.

Pay and resolve modules one at a time. Each output is available immediately for later modules in the same factory or later factories in that connection. A factory that runs no modules does not consume a multiplier, and each factory may run at most once per connection.

Modules owned by different players may share an exoplanet factory. The connected player may operate every module there, pays all running costs and inputs, and receives all outputs regardless of ownership.

Acquiring Exoplanet Modules

This feature is not currently playable because every exoplanet deck and discard pile is empty.

Each exoplanet has its own face-down module deck and discard pile. When a player connects to an exoplanet, they may use one of these methods:

  • Spend one or more Teams. Draw a number of modules equal to one plus the number of Teams spent, keep one, and put the rest in the discard pile.
  • Instead of drawing from the deck, spend one Team to look through the discards and keep one module.

A player may gain no more than one module this way per turn, regardless of which method they use. A kept module is held for construction during this or a later successful connection.

Constructing Modules

  • When a player connects to themself or an exoplanet, they may pay any held module's construction cost to install it at that location.
  • The installed module must match the factory's type. A module installed into an empty factory slot establishes that factory's type.
  • Mark the installed module as belonging to the player who constructed it.
  • Constructing on an exoplanet costs one additional Team on top of the module's regular construction cost.
  • A player may construct any affordable number of held modules before beginning production.
  • Beginning production closes construction for that connection.
  • Unbuilt modules remain held for later connections.

Turn Structure

The repeated connection loop is playable; scoring and the broader game loop remain unresolved:

  1. Resolve a connection selection round.
  2. After each successful self or exoplanet connection, construct held modules or keep them for later.
  3. Run factories or finish the connection without production.
  4. After every eligible player finishes, advance to the next connection selection round.

Connection Selection Round

  1. Each player secretly chooses one selection card.
  2. All players reveal their chosen cards at the same time.
  3. Played cards stay face up on the table and cannot be played again while they remain there.
  4. Resolve non-pause choices first.
  5. Players who revealed pause wait until non-pause choices are visible before choosing a final target card from hand.
  6. Pause choices are chosen, revealed, and resolved simultaneously.
  7. A pause player may choose any target card in hand, including themself or another pause player. The pause card and target card both stay face up after the pause choice is revealed.
  8. Players must choose each other to connect to each other.
  9. If two players chose each other during a resolution step, they connect and may trade.
  10. If exactly one player chose an exoplanet during a resolution step, that player connects to that exoplanet.
  11. If two or more players chose the same exoplanet during a resolution step, none of those players connect to that exoplanet.
  12. Only one player can connect to an exoplanet at a time.
  13. If a player chose themself, that player connects to themself, produces internally, and returns all their face-up played cards to their hand.
  14. A pause player may connect to an exoplanet blocked by non-pause players, but the original claimants remain failed.
  15. If a non-pause player chose a player who revealed pause, that pause player may choose that non-pause player and connect to them.
  16. If a player chose another player who did not choose them, that player fails to connect.
  17. Any player who still failed to connect after pause choices gets a small action as compensation.
